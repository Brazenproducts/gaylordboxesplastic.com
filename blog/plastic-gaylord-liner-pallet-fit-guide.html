Gaylord Box Liner and Pallet Fit: A Spec Reference

Gaylordboxesplastic Editorial • November 2026

Three Interfaces That Decide Whether a Gaylord Survives

A plastic gaylord box rarely fails in the middle of a wall. It fails at an interface: where the box meets the pallet, where the liner meets the box, or where the base meets the forklift. Each of those junctions has a specification, and each one is routinely left to chance. The result is a container rated for a thousand kilograms that bulges on the second tier, or a liner that tears at the fold because nobody checked the corner radius.

This reference covers the dimensions and allowances that matter at each interface.

Matching the Box Footprint to the Pallet

The dominant pallet footprint in North American distribution is the GMA grocery pallet at 48 by 40 inches. A gaylord built for that pallet should sit inside the deck edge, not on it. The practical rule is that the box footprint should be equal to or slightly smaller than the pallet deck, with the load bearing on the deck rather than on the pallet's top boards alone.

Common working footprints for a 48 by 40 pallet run 47.5 by 39.5 inches, leaving a small margin for liner overhang and for the pallet's manufacturing tolerance. If the box is specified at exactly 48 by 40, the liner and any stretch wrap will overhang, and the overhang is what gets caught on conveyor curves and rack uprights.

Overhang and Load Distribution

Overhang is the enemy of stacking strength. A box that extends past the deck edge has no support under the protruding section, so the wall carries a bending moment instead of a compressive load. Under a stacked load that section buckles first, and once one corner yields the whole tier leans.

  • Zero overhang is the target on all four sides.
  • Underhang up to about half an inch is generally acceptable and reduces damage risk.
  • Underhang beyond that concentrates load on the deck's center and can crush the pallet's top boards.

Confirm the pallet specification before the box drawing is released, because changing the box footprint after tooling is far more expensive than changing the pallet order. If the program uses a non-standard pallet, specify the box to that deck rather than to a nominal dimension.

Liner Selection by Function, Not by Habit

The liner does a specific job, and the gauge should follow the job. A liner chosen because it is what the plant has always used is usually either over-specified or under-specified, and both cost money.

  • Dry bulk, no moisture path: a light liner mainly for product containment and dust control.
  • Food contact: a liner with documented food-contact compliance, specified by the resin and any additives rather than by thickness alone.
  • Wet or frozen product: a heavier liner with a sealed base, because the base seam is where cold-chain condensation collects.
  • Abrasive or sharp product: puncture-resistant liner, and a check on the box's internal corner radius.

The liner should be sized to the erected interior with enough slack to reach the rim and fold over, typically several inches of overhang per side. Too little and it drops into the box under load; too much and it fouls the lid or the stretch wrap.

Forklift Entry and Base Clearance

Fork entry is a specification, not an assumption. Confirm the entry height against the actual fork thickness in the fleet, including any wear on the fork tips, and confirm the entry width against fork spacing. A base designed for a pallet truck may not accept a forklift with a thick heel, and the failure mode is a cracked base on the first putaway.

Base clearance also drives the minimum pallet height. The box base has to clear the deck and any pallet top-board gaps, or the load transfers through the base unsupported. This is the point where compatible plastic pallets and the container have to be specified together rather than purchased separately.

Stacking Height and Wall Support

The rated static load assumes a supported wall. In a rack the load path is different from a floor stack, and a container rated for four high on the floor may need internal support at three high in a beam rack. Verify the rating basis — static, dynamic, or rack — before publishing a stacking rule to the warehouse, because the three numbers are not interchangeable.

Putting the Specification on One Drawing

The interfaces only hold if they are documented together. A single drawing should carry the box footprint, the collapsed height, the wall gauge, the liner size and material, the pallet model, the fork entry dimensions, and the rating basis. Elipacko builds plastic gaylord boxes from Elipacko to that kind of drawing, and the value is not the box alone — it is that the four interfaces are specified against each other instead of in four separate purchase orders.
