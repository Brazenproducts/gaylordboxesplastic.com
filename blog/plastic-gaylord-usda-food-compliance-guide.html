Plastic Gaylord Boxes: USDA and FDA Food Compliance Guide

Gaylordboxesplastic Editorial • October 2026

Bulk handling in food and beverage operations lives or dies by compliance documentation. A plastic gaylord box that cannot prove its resin, cleanability, and traceability becomes an audit liability regardless of how well it performs on the dock. This guide walks procurement and quality teams through the USDA and FDA requirements that apply to reusable bulk bins and how to specify a gaylord that passes inspection.

Why Fiber Gaylords Fall Short

Traditional corrugated gaylords absorb moisture, harbor residue, and cannot be sanitized between loads. In a USDA-inspected facility, that makes them a one-way consumable at best and a contamination risk at worst. Plastic gaylords built from food-grade polypropylene change the equation: non-porous walls, wash-down compatibility, and a documented resin chain let the same asset cycle through hundreds of loads while staying inside food-safety rules.

The FDA Resin Requirement

For direct food contact, the polypropylene must comply with FDA 21 CFR 177.1520, the regulation covering olefin polymers in contact with food. Compliance is a property of the resin and any colorants or additives, so a quality file needs documentation from the material source, not just an assurance from the box vendor. Elipacko builds its plastic gaylord boxes from Elipacko using FDA-compliant resin and can supply the grade documentation your HACCP or SQF file requires.

  • Resin compliance: FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 for olefin polymers.
  • Colorants: confirm any pigment is food-contact approved.
  • Traceability: lot or batch records tying the box to its resin.

USDA and Facility Sanitation

USDA oversight in meat and poultry plants focuses on sanitary design and cleanability. A compliant plastic gaylord presents smooth interior surfaces, avoids crevices that trap product, and tolerates the detergents, sanitizers, and temperatures of a plant wash cycle. When specifying, confirm the box survives your actual CIP or manual wash chemistry — polypropylene resists most food-plant sanitizers, but validate against your specific protocol and dwell times.

Specifying a Compliant Bin

A food-grade gaylord program needs more than the right resin. Match the internal dimensions to your product and pallet footprint, confirm the load rating covers your filled weight plus stacking, and decide between collapsible and rigid based on return-leg storage. Documentation Checklist

  • FDA 21 CFR 177.1520 compliance letter for the resin.
  • Food-contact approval for any colorant or additive.
  • Cleanability validation against your plant sanitation protocol.
  • Load rating and stacking data for your filled weight.
  • Lot traceability from box to resin batch.

Collecting these items before the first purchase order turns a bulk-handling upgrade into an audit asset rather than an audit question. A plastic gaylord that arrives with its compliance paperwork already in order protects both the operation and the next inspection.
