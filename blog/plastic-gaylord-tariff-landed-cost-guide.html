Plastic Gaylord Box Tariffs: Modeling Landed Cost by Origin

Gaylordboxesplastic Editorial • November 2026

A Gaylord Is a Classified Article

Procurement teams routinely build a landed cost model for a gaylord box using the raw material price and a freight quote, then get surprised at the port. The container is a finished plastic article, and it is classified as one. That distinction determines the duty rate, and duty is frequently the second largest line in the total cost of a bulk container program after the goods themselves.

This analysis walks through how to model that exposure properly, using the country-of-origin question that now dominates bulk packaging sourcing.

Where a Plastic Gaylord Lands in the Tariff

Plastic gaylord boxes — the large collapsible bulk bins used for food, agricultural, and industrial material handling — generally fall under HTS heading 3923.10 as boxes, cases, and crates of plastics. The base Column 1 General rate on that heading is modest and is not the problem. The problem is the layers that attach to it:

  • Section 301 duties. Chinese-origin plastic packaging was swept into the Section 301 lists, adding 25 percent ad valorem on top of the base rate.
  • Anti-dumping and countervailing duties. Where an AD/CVD order covers the specific product and origin, the deposit rate is collected at entry and can be far larger than the base duty.
  • Merchandise Processing Fee. Ad valorem with minimum and maximum caps.
  • Harbor maintenance and drayage. Small per container, material across a bulk program.

On a gaylord program the duty is assessed per unit, but the unit is large and the annual volume is usually in the thousands. A 25 percent Section 301 layer on a container that lands at sixty dollars is fifteen dollars per box, every box, for the life of the program.

The Thailand and Vietnam Alternative

The Section 301 lists are country-specific. They attach to Chinese-origin goods rather than to the material category, which is why Thailand and Vietnam have absorbed so much of the US bulk packaging volume. As of this writing no anti-dumping order applies to PP gaylord boxes from either origin, so the landed duty position is materially cleaner: base rate plus MPF, without the 25 percent overlay.

The sourcing quality question matters as much as the duty question. A manufacturer that extrudes and fabricates in one facility keeps the country-of-origin chain short and auditable. A Worked Landed Cost Model

The model does not need to be complicated, but it does need every line filled with a number. For each candidate origin, build the following per unit:

  • FOB price at the specified wall gauge and base configuration.
  • Duty at the correct HTS classification, inclusive of any Section 301 or AD/CVD layer.
  • Ocean and inland freight per unit, which depends heavily on how well the boxes nest.
  • Inventory carrying cost at your cost of capital, scaled to the transit window.
  • Damage and rework — the cost of a wall that bows or a base that cracks in transit.

Two inputs change the ranking more than any other: nesting ratio and damage rate. A container that nests flat carries several times the units of one that ships assembled, which can erase a duty advantage entirely. Run the model on both assumptions before deciding.

Freight Economics and the Nesting Ratio

Collapsible gaylords exist because empty volume is expensive. A box that folds to a fraction of its erected height lets a buyer move thousands of units per container, and the freight per unit collapses accordingly. When comparing a Chinese supplier to a Thai or Vietnamese one, confirm the collapsed height and the stacking method rather than assuming parity. A ten centimeter difference in folded height across a full container is a real per unit number.

Documentation and Audit Defense

Origin is a legal claim. Retain a manufacturer's affidavit identifying the extrusion and fabrication locations, keep production records that support the claim, and make sure the certificate of origin matches the invoiced article and the HTS code. Under a bulk program the exposure is concentrated, so an audit that reclassifies a shipment can reach back across entries.

Finally, confirm that the container actually fits the handling equipment. A gaylord is only as good as the compatible plastic pallets under it, and a footprint mismatch creates overhang that damages walls on the first turn. Model the duty, then verify the interface — in that order, but do both before the purchase order goes out.
